📘 List 1: Core AI + Machine Learning (Beginner → Advanced)
These books build the foundation from scratch: math, theory, algorithms, ML, deep learning, up to modern AI research and practice.
Foundations (Math + Algorithms)
** “Artificial Intelligence: A Modern Approach” — Stuart Russell & Peter Norvig
• The standard AI textbook used in universities worldwide — covers search, logic, probabilistic inference, planning, optimization, learning, and reasoning. 
• Great as your first theory backbone.
** “Mathematics for Machine Learning” — Deisenroth, Faisal & Ong
• Linear algebra, probability, statistics, and optimization tailored for ML contexts (not overly abstract).
• Algorithm Design/Analysis (popular texts)
• Books like Introduction to Algorithms (CLRS) or algorithm texts focused on data structures & complexity — not AI‑specific, but essential for core algorithmic thinking.
Intro to Machine Learning
** “Hands‑On Machine Learning with Scikit‑Learn, Keras, and TensorFlow” — Aurélien Géron
• Practical, hands‑on introduction to ML and neural networks with real code examples (Python). 
** “The Hundred‑Page Machine Learning Book” — Andriy Burkov
• Short, concise overview of core ML ideas — great as a second perspective.
Intermediate & Advanced ML / DL
** “Deep Learning” — Ian Goodfellow, Yoshua Bengio & Aaron Courville
• One of the most important deep learning textbooks — covers the math and architectures (CNNs, RNNs, optimization). 
** “Pattern Recognition and Machine Learning” — Christopher M. Bishop
• Classic reference for probabilistic models in ML.
** “Machine Learning: A Probabilistic Perspective” — Kevin P. Murphy
• Very comprehensive, mathematically deep overview of ML — ideal once you have strong foundations.
** “Reinforcement Learning: An Introduction” — Sutton & Barto
• The book on reinforcement learning — critical for game AI if you want agents that learn from interaction. 
🎮 List 2: Game AI & Implementation
These focus on practical game character behavior, real‑time decision systems, and behavior architectures — i.e., the skills you’d use to write AI for actual games.
Note: Game AI is mostly deterministic systems + heuristics + behavior trees, and combines with modern ML (e.g., reinforcement learning) but remains implementation‑centric.
Core Game AI (Traditional)
** “Artificial Intelligence for Games” — Ian Millington & John Funge
• Industry standard for game AI. Covers pathfinding (A*, navigation meshes), behavior trees, state machines, planners, decision trees — all in C++‑friendly context.
** “Programming Game AI by Example” — Mat Buckland
• Hands‑on with examples, including flocking, finite state machines, pathfinding — great before jumping to learned agents.
** “Behavior Trees in Robotics and AI” — A Practical Introduction
• Modern behavior representation widely used in games.
Bringing Learning to Game AI
• Reinforcement Learning texts + resources
• Sutton & Barto (above) is PERFECT for learning agents that adapt within games. 
• You can experiment with Python environments like OpenAI Gym and then port logic into C++ (your strong suit).
• Project‑based books combining ML + games
• No single canonical game‑AI + deep learning book, but online courses and papers (e.g., Deep Reinforcement Learning in Games) complement textbooks.
🧠 List 3: Meta‑Learning & Understanding AI (Context, Ethics, Big‑Picture)
These help you grasp what intelligence means, how AI impacts systems, and how it differs from traditional programming — valuable for job interviews and high‑level design thinking.
** “The Master Algorithm” — Pedro Domingos
• Explains five major paradigms of ML and unifying perspectives. 
** “You Look Like a Thing and I Love You” — Janelle Shane
• A fun, intuitive look at how ML systems behave — not technical but great for intuition. 
There are also popular books like Life 3.0 or Superintelligence that explore the impact and philosophy of AI — good for interviews or thinking, not core skills.
